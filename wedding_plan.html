The day, in order

One adat ritual kept — the siraman — then a full wedding day
The day before

  • Siraman — the bathing

    A purification at home with flower water drawn from seven sources, poured by the elders, to simple gendhing. It closes with sungkeman to the parents and the symbolic dodol dawet. This is the one pre-wedding adat we're keeping.

The wedding day

  • Ijab · Akad Nikah — the marriage contract

    The Islamic vows. Held at Rooang for Option B, or at home first then Rooang for Option A. A day-of coordinator and MC keep the rundown on time.

  • Panggih — the meeting of the two

    Bride and groom meet as married: balangan suruh, the egg, sindur, kacar-kucur, feeding one another, and a final sungkeman.

  • Resepsi — the reception

    At Rooang Dharmawangsa: subtle, elegant decor dressed in white melati, seated catering, live music, and an MC to carry the evening — then a night in the bridal suite.

Kept deliberately light. We've set aside the midodareni seclusion night and the tarub gate-canopy to keep the pre-wedding subtle — just the siraman remains. Say the word and either can come back as a costed add-on.
A Bugis thread through a Javanese spine. The adat follows the bride's Javanese tradition. The groom's Buginese side can be woven in gracefully — a Bugis accent in his attire, a family blessing, a nod in the music. A separate Bugis ngunduh mantu in Sulawesi isn't costed here.
